      01 · The Problem & Who Needs This

      Every new table means days of manual simulation setup—so engineers skip it and risk real hardware.

      When bringing up a bimanual manipulation station in a lab, warehouse, or factory, the bottleneck isn't the robot controller—it's getting the real workspace into a physics simulator before running untested motions on hardware.

      Target User · Robotics Bring-Up Engineers

      Days Lost to Tape-Measure URDFs

      Every time a two-arm station moves to a new workbench, an engineer has to measure the table, measure every monitor and fixture, hand-author an MJCF/URDF scene file, and hand-tune pregrasp clearances and handover waypoints. Because that takes days, teams skip simulation and test directly on $30k arms.

      Target User · Lab & Factory Cell Operators

      Cluttered Desks Cause Hidden Collisions

      Real workspaces aren't empty tables—they have stacked monitors, laptops, stands, and cables. In our unpatched Attempt 1, the can actually lands within 5.6 mm of the goal, but an arm plows through the monitor at 95.5 N (2.87 mm inside). Without fitted obstacle collision boxes, a naive check would call that a pass and break the screen.

      Target User · Embodied AI & World Model Teams

      Models Shouldn't Grade Their Own Homework

      Google's showcase for Argon 3D worlds (Desk Racer) uses a critic model to rate screenshots of a game on a desk. Same input here, different finish line: in robotics, a VLM cannot declare its own success. The model proposes; MuJoCo 3.17 decides against fixed newton and millimeter thresholds.

      02 · Closed-Loop Architecture

      The Model Proposes. MuJoCo Decides.

      ArgonTwin separates multimodal spatial reasoning (gemini-4-argon) from deterministic physical verification (sim.py + schemas.judge).

      1. Deterministic Referee

      schemas.judge() evaluates measured MuJoCo numbers against frozen thresholds in config.py: handover completed, not dropped, within 3 cm of target, at rest, table force ≤ 25 N, obstacle force ≤ 5 N, and obstacle penetration ≤ 2 mm.

      2. Zero-Hint Visual Critique

      When an attempt fails, argon.critique() sends 20 rendered keyframes across 4 cameras plus the measured metrics to gemini-4-argon via multimodal generateContent. No passing coordinates are ever leaked in the prompt.

      3. Typed Pydantic PlanPatch

      Argon returns a validated CritiqueBody citing exact failure keyframes (e.g. attempt1_handover_front) and a PlanPatch updating handover_point_m, lift_height_m, or clearances.

      4. Human-in-the-Loop Control

      Operators can inspect collision outlines (O), switch to the moving gripper camera (C), or click Edit plan / Break the plan to place the handover behind the screens and watch Argon repair it live.

      03 · Real-World Capture → 3D Gaussian Splat → RANSAC Collision Twin

      How We Built the 3D Desk & Segmented Celsius Can from a 10.4 s Phone Video

      A 3D Gaussian Splat looks photorealistic, but MuJoCo cannot collide robot arms against fuzzy Gaussian clouds. Here is the exact 4-stage pipeline we ran at SHACK15 today to bridge pixels, 3D Gaussians, and millimeter-accurate collision boxes.

      8 Registered Camera Views Around the Desk (Click any angle to seek video):
      Local pycolmap & Sparse 3D Points: Runs on MacBook CPU (capture/prepare_local.py). SIFT matches visual corners across all 52/52 frames and triangulates 20,762 3D points (filtered to 3,456 low-error starting points).
      Open-Source gsplat on Colab NVIDIA T4 GPU (Custom CUDA): Each 3D Gaussian is a 32-byte 3D ellipsoid (position, 3D scale, quaternion rotation, color, opacity). Custom CUDA kernels train 68,192 Gaussians in 10.0 s on an NVIDIA Tesla T4, cropped to 17,157 desk Gaussians (desk.splat) + 943 Celsius-can Gaussians (celsius-can.splat).
      2,000-Iteration RANSAC Plane Fit: capture/fit_collision_scene.py fits planes to the tracked 3D points per panel (1.6–3.7 mm RMS error, inflated 8 mm) to produce 5 solid MuJoCo collision boxes (upper_screen, lower_screen, laptop_base, left_screen, left_base).
